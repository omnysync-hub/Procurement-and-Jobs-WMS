Part 1

Procurement

How a purchase happens — from someone raising a need, to the bill actually getting paid.

Overview

The whole journey, in one line

Every purchase goes through the same nine stops, in the same order. Nothing skips ahead, and every handoff is recorded.

Step 1

Vendors and items on file

Two lists everything else depends on: who you buy from, and what you buy. Get these right once, and every later step just pulls from here.

About each vendor

  • Vendor code, name, address, contact person
  • Tax number
  • How and when they get paid
  • Currency and bank details
  • What kind of vendor — goods, service, etc.
  • Active, blocked, or blacklisted

About each item

  • Item code and description
  • Unit of measure
  • Category
  • Standard and last-paid price
  • Preferred vendor(s)
  • When to reorder
Step 2

Someone asks for something

This is where every purchase starts. Someone says what they need, and it goes for approval before anyone buys anything.

What's on the request

  • Who's asking, from which department
  • When it's needed, how urgent it is
  • What item, how much, roughly what it costs
  • Which budget it comes out of
  • Any attachments — a spec sheet, a drawing

What can happen to it

Draft Sent for approval Approved Turned down Turned into an order

One request can turn into several orders, and several requests can be combined into one order.

Step 4

Placing the order

The official commitment to a vendor. Created straight from the approved request, and tracked all the way to done.

One-off order Standing agreement — for repeat buys Order for a service

Top of the order

  • Order number, vendor, dates
  • Payment terms, currency, where it ships

Line by line

  • Item, quantity, price, discount, tax
  • Delivery dates, which request it's from
Step 5

Checking what arrives

What actually showed up, checked against what was ordered. Partial deliveries are normal — what matters is what happens next based on quality.

Step 6

Paying the bill — the 3-way check

The most important safeguard in the whole process. The bill only gets paid once the order, the delivery, and the invoice all agree with each other.

Part 2

Jobs

How a job moves from the first phone call to a closed, verified record — and the small steps each person takes along the way.

Overview

A job's life, start to finish

A job runs from Created to Verified in one line. If the work can't finish the same day, it pauses — and only picks back up once the technician's expenses for the day are settled.

Step 1

Who touches the job, and when

Dispatcher opens it, the technician does the work, the accountant settles the cash, the call center checks in with the customer, and the admin gives the final okay.
